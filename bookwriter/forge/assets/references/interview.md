# The interview

Forty-odd questions in eight blocks. Ask them in batches of four to six, in
this order — later blocks depend on earlier answers. Every question has a
default in brackets: propose it, so the author can say "as you prefer" and keep
moving. Record every answer; they all end up in `MASTER_SPEC.md` or
`book.yaml`.

A book fails in one of three ways: it is written for nobody in particular, it
is an unreadable object, or it never reaches a reader. The three groups of
questions below exist to prevent each of those.

---

## Block 1 — The book and its purpose

1. **What is this book?** One sentence, the kind that would appear on the back
   cover. Push until it is one sentence, not a paragraph.
2. **What genre or form?** Practical manual · technical handbook · essay ·
   academic monograph · memoir · novel · short fiction · poetry · workbook ·
   textbook · illustrated book · reference. The answer changes the structure,
   the apparatus and the trim.
3. **What is the thesis?** The claim the whole book defends, or — in fiction —
   the question the story asks. If there is no answer, the book has no spine.
4. **What changes in the reader?** Something they know, can do, believe, or
   feel that they could not before.
5. **Why this book rather than the ones that exist?** Name two or three
   competitors and say what they miss. If nothing is missing, the honest answer
   may be a different book.
6. **Whose book is it?** Sole author · co-authors · ghostwritten · an
   organisation. Affects the byline, the copyright line and the voice.

## Block 2 — The reader

7. **Who reads this?** Not "everyone". Age range, occupation, situation.
8. **What do they already know?** The floor: what you can assume without
   explaining. Getting this wrong is the most common failure in non-fiction.
9. **Why do they open it?** A problem they have, a curiosity, an obligation
   (a course), a pleasure.
10. **Where and how do they read?** Continuously, or dipping in for reference?
    On paper at a desk, on a phone on a train, aloud to someone? This decides
    chapter length, cross-references and whether an index earns its pages.
11. **What objection do they bring?** The scepticism to disarm in the first
    ten pages.
12. **What must they never feel?** Talked down to, lectured, sold to, judged.
13. **Any secondary audience?** Buyers who are not readers — parents, trainers,
    procurement — change the cover and the blurb, not the text.

## Block 3 — The object

14. **Paper, ebook, or both?** [both] Print alone means the layout can be
    fixed; ebook alone means fixed layout is meaningless. Both means writing
    once and composing twice, which is what this pipeline does.
15. **Trim size?** [A5 · 148×210 mm] The common ones:
    - A5 148×210 — European default for manuals and essays
    - B-format 129×198 — UK paperback, fiction
    - 5×8 in 127×203 — compact US trade
    - 5.5×8.5 in 140×216 — KDP digest, non-fiction
    - 6×9 in 152×229 — KDP trade, the safest US default
    - 7×10 in 178×254 — workbooks, anything with tables or exercises
    - A4 210×297 — **not a book format.** If they ask for it, they usually mean
      a printable PDF workbook. Confirm before accepting it.
16. **Target extent?** Words, or pages, or both. Rough conversion at A5:
    330–360 words per page of running text. 35,000 words ≈ 130 pages;
    90,000 ≈ 300 pages.
17. **Interior in black or colour?** [black] Colour multiplies print cost per
    page. If there are charts, they must survive in greyscale.
18. **Binding?** [perfect bound paperback] Paperback · hardback · spiral (for
    workbooks that must lie flat) · saddle-stitch (under 48 pages).
19. **Illustrations, photographs, diagrams, tables?** How many, who supplies
    them, and do the rights exist? Ask about rights explicitly — it is the
    thing authors forget until the printer asks.
20. **Ebook fixed-layout or reflowable?** [reflowable] Fixed layout only for
    picture books and heavily designed pages; it reads badly on phones.

## Block 4 — Publication and channel

21. **How is it published?** [KDP] Amazon KDP · IngramSpark · a local printer ·
    a publisher · PDF distributed directly · internal circulation only.
    Each wants different files: KDP wants interior and cover separately,
    a printer often wants a single PDF with crop marks and bleed.
22. **Print run?** Print-on-demand (one at a time) or an offset run (hundreds).
    Offset means talking to a prepress operator about colour profiles.
23. **ISBN?** [placeholder] Their own, the store's free one, or none. It goes
    on the copyright page and in the ebook metadata.
24. **Imprint name?** [Edizione dell'autore / Independently published]
25. **Price and territory?** Only if they want it printed anywhere in the book.
26. **Copyright and licence?** [all rights reserved] Or a Creative Commons
    licence, which must be named exactly.
27. **Deadline?** A date changes what is worth attempting.

## Block 5 — Identity of the volume

28. **Title.** If undecided, defer it — but not past Phase 5, because the cover
    needs it.
29. **Subtitle.** In non-fiction this does the selling: it says what the reader
    gets. Keep it under twelve words.
30. **Author name as printed.** Real name, pseudonym, or none.
31. **Dedication?** [none] One or two lines.
32. **Epigraph?** [none] A quotation opening the book — check that quoting it
    is permitted; song lyrics and living poets are the usual traps.
33. **Author biography.** **Ask for this properly.** It is not an afterthought:
    it appears in the back matter, on the cover flap or back cover, in the
    ebook metadata, and on the store page — it is the single most-read piece of
    text after the blurb. Collect: a 40-word version and a 120-word version,
    what qualifies them to write this book, what they want the reader to do
    next (site, newsletter, other titles), and whether a photograph exists.
    If they resist writing it, draft one from what you know and let them cut.
34. **Acknowledgements?** [none] Names, spelled as they want them.
35. **Back cover copy?** [to write] The blurb, the biography, the barcode.
    Usually the last thing done and the first thing read.

## Block 6 — Voice and texture

36. **Reference authors.** One to three, and specifically what to take from
    each: sentence rhythm, vocabulary, how they open a chapter, how they use
    evidence. "Write like X" is useless; "borrow X's habit of opening on a
    concrete scene" is actionable.
37. **Register.** Formal · neutral · conversational · technical. And: do you
    address the reader as *tu*, *lei*, *you*, or not at all?
38. **Person and tense.** First or third; present or past. In non-fiction, is
    there an "I" with a biography, or an impersonal voice?
39. **Density.** How much room for digression, anecdote, humour. A dense
    reference book and a discursive essay are different products.
40. **Anything to avoid?** Topics, framings, words, examples. Ask explicitly:
    this is where a book gets into trouble late.

## Block 7 — Apparatus and structure

41. **Parts, or just chapters?** [parts if more than eight chapters]
42. **Chapter anatomy.** Does every chapter follow the same skeleton — opening,
    development, examples, exercises, summary — or vary freely? Uniform helps
    a manual and kills an essay.
43. **Exercises or practical apparatus?** [none] If yes: at the end of each
    chapter, inline, or gathered at the back; and what an exercise looks like.
44. **Citations.** [author-year, one bibliography at the back] Alternatives:
    footnotes, endnotes per chapter, no visible citations at all. Decide now —
    retrofitting is expensive.
45. **Evidence standard.** Peer-reviewed only · reputable secondary sources
    acceptable · experience and anecdote allowed if labelled. See
    `research.md`.
46. **Which back matter?** Glossary · appendices · index · bibliography ·
    author note · acknowledgements · further reading. A real index needs page
    references and is done last, on the composed pages; offer it only if the
    book is a reference work.
47. **Front matter?** Preface · foreword by someone else · notice or
    disclaimer · introduction. A disclaimer is not decorative in health, legal,
    financial or psychological subjects.

## Block 8 — Research and production

48. **Should I research online?** [yes, for outline and content] Ask what for:
    verifying facts · finding studies · surveying the competition · gathering
    cases and examples · checking current data. See `research.md`.
49. **Existing material?** Notes, drafts, transcripts, a previous edition, a
    blog to mine. Ask before writing anything from scratch.
50. **Who reviews, and when?** Chapter by chapter, or the whole draft. Silence
    until page 300 is how books get rewritten twice.
51. **The cover.** Covered in Phase 5 of the skill, and worth raising here so
    it is not a surprise: does the artwork exist, who makes it, and by when?

---

## Closing the interview

Summarise in one screen: form, reader, thesis, extent, trim, channel, voice,
apparatus, research, cover. Ask one question — "is this the book?" — and only
then write `MASTER_SPEC.md`.

If the author is impatient with the interview, compress it: blocks 1, 2, 3 and
the four load-bearing questions are the minimum. Everything else can carry a
default into the spec and be corrected there, where changing it is still cheap.
