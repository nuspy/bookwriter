# Structure: what goes where

## Front matter, in order

| Page | Element | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| i | Half title | Title alone, on a recto |
| ii | blank (or frontispiece) | |
| iii | Title page | Author, title, subtitle, imprint |
| iv | Copyright page | On the verso, always |
| v | Dedication | Optional, recto, generous white space |
| vi | blank | |
| vii | Contents | Recto |
| — | Foreword | By someone other than the author |
| — | Preface | By the author, about the book's making |
| — | Notice / disclaimer | Required in health, legal, financial, psychological subjects |
| — | Introduction | Part of the book's argument, not of its apparatus |

Preface and introduction are different things. A preface explains how the book
came to exist; an introduction begins the book. If there is only one, it is
almost always an introduction.

The **copyright page** carries: title and subtitle, the copyright year and
rights holder, the rights statement, the edition, the ISBN, the imprint, and
any legal notice. Keep it small, one screen at most.

## Body

**Parts** group chapters when there are more than eight, or when the book has
distinct movements. A part opener is a recto carrying the part number, the
title, and optionally a paragraph placing it. Parts appear in the contents.

**Chapter anatomy** for non-fiction, as a default to vary deliberately:

1. Opening — a concrete hook, the question the chapter answers, why it matters
2. Development — the substance, in sections of 400 to 900 words
3. Application — examples, cases, exercises, protocols
4. Close — what to retain, and the bridge to what follows

Heading levels: the chapter title, then two or at most three levels inside it.
A fourth level usually means the chapter should have been two chapters.

## Back matter, in order

| Element | Notes |
|---|---|
| Appendices | Material that would interrupt the argument |
| Glossary | If the book coins or borrows more than a dozen terms |
| Bibliography | One list, alphabetical, merged from the chapter sources |
| Notes | If using endnotes rather than footnotes |
| Index | Only for reference works — needs composed pages, done last |
| Author biography | See below |
| Acknowledgements | Last, or first in the front matter; never both |
| Colophon | Edition, fonts, printing |

### The author biography

Treat it as a component of the book, not a formality. It appears in four
places, each wanting a different length:

- **Back matter**, 100 to 150 words: who the author is, what qualifies them for
  *this* book, one human detail, and what the reader should do next.
- **Back cover**, 40 to 60 words: the same, compressed, in the third person.
- **Ebook metadata**, one or two sentences.
- **Store page**, which the author maintains but which should match.

Collect it during the interview (block 5, question 33). If the author resists
writing it, draft both lengths from what you know and let them cut — people
edit a draft far more readily than they face a blank page.

Ask whether a photograph exists and at what resolution. Print wants 300 dpi;
a phone selfie will not do.

## Bibliography

One list at the back, alphabetical by author surname, in the style chosen
during the interview (APA 7 by default for non-fiction). The composer collects
per-chapter reference lists — anything under a final `## Note bibliografiche`
or `## References` heading — deduplicates them and merges them. Keep the
per-chapter lists while writing: they are how you know a chapter is sourced.

## Cross-references

Refer to chapters by number and title, never by page: pages move between
editions, and in the ebook there are no pages at all.
